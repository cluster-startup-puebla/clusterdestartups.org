## The Cold AI War has a second front. And nobody is talking about it.

This week Mrinank Sharma — head of the Safeguards Research team at Anthropic, with a doctorate in machine learning from Oxford — resigned in public. His letter went viral, with more than a million views on X.

His work included understanding AI sycophancy, building defenses against AI-assisted bioterrorism, putting them into production, and writing one of the industry's first AI safety cases. His last project: investigating how AI assistants can distort our humanity.

In his words:

> The world is in peril. And not just from AI, or bioweapons, but from a whole series of interconnected crises unfolding in this very moment. We seem to be approaching a threshold where our wisdom must grow in equal measure to our capacity to affect the world, or we will face the consequences.

That same week the WHO launched its 2026 emergency appeal: nearly one billion dollars to respond to 36 health emergencies at once, including 14 Grade 3 emergencies, the organization's highest level of response. In 2025 humanitarian funding fell below 2016 levels, and they could reach only a third of the 81 million people who needed help. The source is the WHO Health Emergency Appeal 2026, 3 February 2026.

Coincidence? Probably. Curious? Yes.

Now put these figures next to each other.

The picture that went viral between 2025 and 2026:

- Measles in 179 countries: more than 552,000 suspected cases in 2025. In the Americas, a 32-fold increase over 2024.
- PAHO warned in December 2025 about the global rise of influenza A(H3N2) subclade K.
- Mpox continues, with peaks in Africa (Kenya, Zambia).
- Nipah virus was active in India in January 2026. The WHO keeps it as a priority pathogen with pandemic potential.
- 239 million people need humanitarian assistance in 2026.
- More than 6,600 health centers closed or cut services for lack of funds, leaving 53 million people without care. Source: WHO.

The AI and biosecurity picture, over the same period:

- In February 2025 OpenAI published that its Deep Research tool can help experts with operational planning to reproduce a known biological threat. Source: CSIS, August 2025.
- In July 2025 OpenAI classified its ChatGPT Agent as high capability for biological risk: a category for capabilities that significantly increase existing risk vectors for severe harm. Source: ChatGPT Agent System Card, OpenAI.
- In October 2025 a study showed that AI can design dangerous DNA sequences that evade biosecurity systems. Eric Horvitz, of Microsoft, warned that those reformulated sequences passed the safety filters. Source: NPR, October 2025.
- Anthropic assessed that its own Claude 3.7 Sonnet gives better advice on key steps of a weaponization pathway and makes fewer mistakes on the critical steps. Source: CSIS, citing Anthropic evaluations.
- The Council on Strategic Risks warned that AI's ability to challenge the relationships between sequence, structure and function of biomolecules is pushing current biosecurity methods to their limits. Source: CSR, December 2025.
- RAND and NTI brought 25 experts together at the AI Action Summit 2025. The conclusion: current governance frameworks were not designed for these capabilities and need an urgent update. Source: RAND Europe, 2025.

The asymmetry almost nobody discusses:

- Global military spending in 2024: 2.72 trillion dollars. The largest real increase since the end of the Cold War, 9.4 percent. Source: SIPRI, April 2025.
- The United States proposed the first one-trillion-dollar defense budget for 2026, with 179 billion for military research and development alone, 27 percent more than the year before. Source: CSIS / Global X ETFs.
- Global military spending is projected to reach 3 trillion before 2026 ends.
- For its 36 health emergencies, the WHO is asking for one billion. That is 0.04 percent of what the world spends on weapons.
- WHO director-general Tedros said that about a quarter of a billion people live in humanitarian crises, while global military spending exceeds 2.5 trillion a year. Source: UN News, February 2026.

I am not saying there is a conspiracy. I am saying there is a brutal asymmetry that deserves attention.

Mrinank did not leave for the money. He did not leave for another AI company. He left to study poetry. He wants to place poetic truth beside scientific truth, as equally valid ways of knowing.

What weighs most in his letter is this:

> Throughout my time here, I have repeatedly seen how hard it is to truly let our values govern our actions. I have seen it in myself, inside the organization, where we constantly face pressures to set aside what matters most, and also in society at large.

The person building the walls against AI-assisted bioterrorism feels that business pressure is beating ethics. And he is not the first: a former member of OpenAI's Superalignment team resigned after realizing the company was prioritizing shiny new products over the safety of the people using them.

Meanwhile Dario Amodei — chief executive of Anthropic, the company Mrinank left — warned the United States Senate that AI has the potential to greatly widen the range of actors with the technical capacity to carry out a large-scale biological attack. Source: Belfer Center, Harvard.

Three trends converge:

1. Preventable diseases resurge because of vaccination gaps, while health funding is cut.
2. Biological AI is moving faster than the governance frameworks designed to contain it.
3. The talent that should be defending us is leaving the ship because it feels its integrity is at stake.

Mrinank closed his letter with a poem by William Stafford, "The Way It Is": there is a thread you follow. It goes among things that change. But it doesn't change. While you hold it you can't get lost.

Today's question is not whether AI will replace you at work. It is whether we are letting it replace us in our priorities.

The Cold AI War is not won with firewalls. It is won by informed citizens who ask the uncomfortable questions.
