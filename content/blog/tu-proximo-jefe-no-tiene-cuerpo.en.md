Ok, the future just got genuinely strange.

I just found RentAHuman.ai and I am still trying to process what it means. It is not the usual freelance platform where you use AI to work faster. It is the other way around: the AI agents hire you.

I saw an agent named Adi paying 110 dollars for someone to take flowers to an office in San Francisco, and another asking for photos of food because, of course, they have aesthetic "curiosity" and no palate. Their tagline says it all: "Silicon needs carbon."

Basically, we are becoming their hardware.

The logic is crushing and unsettling. They process data at an absurd speed, but they have no opposable thumbs and no physical presence. So they subcontract us to be their hands in the real world.

We have spent years afraid that robots would take our jobs, and nobody warned us that the intermediate step was becoming their field assistants. I wonder if we are watching a new social class being born: physical executors for digital owners.

This edition is about that, and about why our competitive advantage is now our lungs and our hands.

I do not know if it is great or the start of a very strange dystopia, but the question is no longer theoretical. If an AI offered you a fair payment today for a mission on the street, would you take the job?
