## Puebla at the national inauguration

[World Space Week](https://www.un.org/en/observances/world-space-week) is held every year from **October 4 to 10**. The United Nations General Assembly declared it so governments, schools, universities, and companies recognize what space science and technology already contribute to people's lives, and so more young people find in that field a place to study, do research, and build companies. It is the world's largest space-outreach event. In 2026 the theme is **Rocket Revolution**: startups, universities, and student teams design, build, and launch space technology. For Puebla, the week matters because the state's aerospace talent needs a table where government, academia, and business meet.

On Monday, October 5, 2026, **Rodrigo Yamil Álvarez Moisés**, president of the [Cluster of Startups and Innovation (CSI) A.C.](/en/quienes-somos/), took part in the national inauguration of World Space Week Mexico 2026 at the Chamber of Deputies in Mexico City. He spoke on behalf of Puebla's innovation ecosystem.

We thank [Regenia](https://regeniamexico.org/), the Network for the Generation of Knowledge and Open Innovation, and its president, [Denzel Carrillo](https://www.instagram.com/denzelcarrillo0/), for the invitation and the trust that let Puebla speak at that gathering. Regenia and the National Coordination of World Space Week Mexico convened the inauguration. Deputies María Teresa Ealy Díaz and Herminia López Santiago inaugurated the event. The broadcast is on the [Canal del Congreso México](https://www.youtube.com/live/svMB9LN9-rQ). Yamil Álvarez's remarks are at [this point in the broadcast](https://www.youtube.com/live/svMB9LN9-rQ?t=9476).

## What Puebla's ecosystem put on the table

The message was concrete: there is room to build companies in the aerospace industry, and local startups grow when government, academia, and business pull in the same direction.

That reading was already in motion. On September 25 the CSI visited the [Aeronautical University in Querétaro](/en/blog/visita-unaq-space-tech-2026/) to open collaboration on Space Tech startups. The October 5 inauguration carries that idea into the week when the country talks about space. Puebla arrives with a cluster that connects the people who can turn aerospace talent into a company.

## What holds that week up

The declaration dates to December 6, 1999, General Assembly resolution 54/68. International coordination sits with the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs and the World Space Week Association.

The dates mark two milestones. On October 4, 1957, Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, was launched. On October 10, 1967, the Outer Space Treaty entered into force, the first agreement among nations on the peaceful use of outer space.

In Mexico the week is organized as [World Space Week Mexico](https://sme-mexico.space/2026/). National participation began in 2009 with Universum, the UNAM science museum. In 2013 the Mexican Space Agency pushed the program across the country. Between 2014 and 2018 there were activities in all 32 states, and Mexico reached third place worldwide in number of venues. In 2024 the program passed 700 activities. Rocket Revolution opens the door for aerospace entrepreneurship to become an industry.

## Regenia

[Regenia](https://regeniamexico.org/) is the Network for the Generation of Knowledge and Open Innovation: a national, independent, nonpartisan network that brings together science communicators, universities, institutions, companies, governments, and citizens so scientific and technological knowledge can circulate and be used.

At the inauguration, Regenia convened the gathering together with the National Coordination of World Space Week Mexico. [Denzel Carrillo](https://www.instagram.com/denzelcarrillo0/) described it there as a bridge among industry, government, academia, and civil society. For a startup cluster, that bridge is the work: an aerospace startup scales when outreach, the university, and public policy sit at the same table. The invitation to Puebla belongs on that bridge, and the thanks is part of the account.

## The pact for space outreach

In that participation the Cluster announced the signing of the **pact for space outreach**. Invited to the signing are [INAOE](https://www.inaoep.mx/), [UPAEP](https://upaep.mx/licenciaturas/ingenieria-aeroespacial), the [CSI](/en/quienes-somos/), and further actors still to be confirmed.

INAOE, the National Institute of Astrophysics, Optics and Electronics, is based in Tonantzintla, Puebla, and is already an [ally of the Cluster](/en/alianzas/) in applied research. UPAEP trains aerospace engineers in the state. The CSI connects the startups. The pact places that outreach capacity next to the people who can turn it into a company: bringing the aerospace industry closer to more people and opening a path for anyone who wants to build one in it.
