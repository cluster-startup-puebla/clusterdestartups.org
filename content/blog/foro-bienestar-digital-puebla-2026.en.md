On Monday, October 12, 2026, the [Puebla State Congress](https://congresopuebla.gob.mx/) held the Digital Wellbeing Forum. The meeting opened a dialogue on regulating mobile devices in schools and on the responsible use of technology. Deputies Ana Laura Gómez Ramírez, chair of the Business Advisory Council, and María Soledad Amieva Zamora, chair of the Academic Advisory Council, convened it.

The [Cluster of Startups and Innovation (CSI) A.C.](/en/quienes-somos/) took part on two levels. Its president, Rodrigo Yamil Álvarez Moisés, joined the presidium. Its vice president, Álvaro Castillo Carreño, took part in the technology and artificial intelligence table.

That conversation already had a precedent in the same building. On September 14, at the advisory councils' session, the Cluster had put digital wellbeing on the agenda: regulating devices in schools and the responsible use of information technology and artificial intelligence. That account is in [the note on that session](/en/blog/congreso-puebla-competitividad-2026/).

Deputy María Soledad Amieva Zamora set the day's frame: the purpose is not to ban phones, but to teach people to use them with judgment and to set rules by age and context, with moments of disconnection, data protection, source verification, and routes to ask for help. Deputy Ana Laura Gómez Ramírez said the contributions will be taken into account in building the legal framework. The next day she [reported](https://enlineapuebla.com/al-dia/alistan-iniciativa-sobre-bienestar-digital-anuncia-la-diputada-ana-laura-gomez/) that the conclusions of the forum and of the earlier meetings will be gathered into a base document, from which the digital wellbeing bill will start. In that account she spoke of eight thematic tables —health, technology, law, ethics, and multidisciplinary work— with researchers, academics, teachers, parents, and students.

At the technology and artificial intelligence table the dialogue did not stop at the handset. The underlying question was whether the problem is the phone or the design of platforms built to capture and hold attention: infinite scroll, notifications, and recommendation algorithms. From there the table moved to how a school can apply a restriction without closing off pedagogical use, and without opening the door to social media and entertainment. It also reviewed how a school should respond when a digital risk starts inside the building and continues outside: non-consensual sharing of images, cyberbullying, or sextortion.

Generative artificial intelligence had a section of its own, because a law centered on phones does not cover it. The table named fake or sexualized images of classmates, companion chatbots, and homework completed by a model, with privacy as the safeguard. The practical close was different: how a school and families keep a reliable channel in an earthquake, a security incident, or a medical emergency if the phone is not at hand.

Before the tables, the [Congress bulletin](https://www.congresopuebla.gob.mx/index.php?id=28290:congreso-del-estado-realiza-foro-para-el-bienestar-digital&option=com_k2&view=item) recorded statements of support from Héctor Guillermo Silva Galindo, acting head of the Secretariat of Science, Humanities, Technology and Innovation; Jorge Medina Delgadillo, rector of [UPAEP](https://upaep.mx/); Alfredo Gómez Palacios and José Luis González Morales, general secretaries of the SNTE; Natalie Hoyos López, head of the Information and Communication System; and José Enrique Martínez Peláez, undersecretary of Compulsory Education.

For the Cluster, the forum continues the line opened in September. Technology enters the law as a tool, with rules that protect children and adolescents, and with an explicit place for what artificial intelligence has already changed in school.

We thank the State Congress and deputies Ana Laura Gómez Ramírez and María Soledad Amieva Zamora for the space to carry the voice of the startup and innovation ecosystem.
