On Saturday, October 10, 2026, [World Space Week Mexico](https://www.sme-mexico.space/2026/) closed in Puebla. At [UPAEP](https://upaep.mx/), engineer Mario M. Arreola Santander, national coordinator of the week, handed the state the torch rocket. That handover opened the **Year of Space in the State of Puebla 2026-2027**. Héctor Guillermo Silva Galindo, acting head of the Secretariat of Science, Humanities, Technology and Innovation, received it on behalf of the state government.

The National Coordination and [Regenia](https://regeniamexico.org/), the network chaired by [Denzel Carrillo](https://www.instagram.com/denzelcarrillo0/), convened the closing. The [Cluster of Startups and Innovation (CSI) A.C.](/en/quienes-somos/) was there through its president, Rodrigo Yamil Álvarez Moisés. Five days earlier, at the national inauguration, Puebla had spoken in the Chamber of Deputies. That account is in [the October 5 note](/en/blog/semana-mundial-espacio-puebla-2026/). On Saturday the week returned to the city that will carry the year.

The morning included student projects — Milvus, Aerodesign, Meva, and Gxiba-1 — and, on Esplanade C, a water-rocket competition for high school and undergraduate teams. The meaning of the day sat in what opened after the applause. The conveners issued the Manifesto of collaboration toward the **Pact for the Development of the Aerospace Sector in Puebla**. It is a public statement of intent. The signing is set for November 30, 2026.

The manifesto invites more people and institutions to bring Puebla into the aerospace value chain. The work it proposes rests on five lines.

The first is science outreach: bringing space science and technology closer to society and awakening scientific vocations. The second is the reconfiguration of industry in Puebla, so the state's productive base can enter the aerospace value chain. The third is support for technology entrepreneurship in space tech, so scientific and technological projects become startups with a business model. The fourth brings education closer to what private industry needs, and aligns talent training with that demand. The fifth is national and international linkage: connecting Puebla's ecosystem with the aerospace sector in Mexico and abroad.

Universities, research centers, chambers, clusters, companies, and communities can join. Prior aerospace experience is not required. Adhesion is in writing to the Cluster, with a representative and a contribution, by November 13, through [contact](/en/contacto/). Whoever writes in takes part in drafting the pact.

The closing brought together the manifesto's conveners. In their remarks they expressed support for the project. Present were Rosa María Cantón Croda, UPAEP vice rector for research; David Sánchez de la Llave, director general of [INAOE](https://www.inaoep.mx/); Marco Antonio Molina Sánchez, for the Secretariat of Economic Development and Labor; José Antonio Silva Hernández, director of the Capital of Technology and Sustainability; Mónica Doger Ramírez, director of the Central Zone Automotive Cluster; Juan Alberto Vázquez, vice president of committees at CANACINTRA Puebla; José Pablo Nuño de la Parra, president of Sintonía; and Argel Vega, for Yankuilotl. With them were SECIHTI, the CSI, the National Coordination, and Regenia. None of them closes the path. The Year of Space in the State of Puebla begins with that open alliance, and with the invitation for other partners to widen it before the signing.

We thank UPAEP, Regenia, and Mario Arreola Santander for bringing the national closing of the week to Puebla.
