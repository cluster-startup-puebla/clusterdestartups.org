## B2B industrial startups in front of the supply chain

The Cluster of Startups and Innovation (CSI) A.C. from Puebla took part with **stand 777** in the **Industrial Clusters Pavilion** at Mexico's Industry Supply Chain (CAPIM) 2026, held August 12–13 at the Centro Expositor de Puebla, in its fourth edition and second consecutive year in the state.

The Cluster's goal was to present **B2B industrial startups** to buyers and suppliers of the national supply chain: the gathering — organized by CAPIM, the Cadena de Proveedores de la Industria en México — convoked more than 7,000 companies from 16 countries, with over 2,256 active purchase requirements on the platform and a linked business opportunity above 15.7 billion US dollars, the highest amount recorded by CAPIM in its twenty-year history.

## The presented startups

Six affiliated companies — selected for supplying the Puebla industry directly — exhibited their capabilities at the stand:

- **Karggu** — logistics: a platform connecting parcel carriers, freight transport and Carta Porte compliance, with tracking, per-customer balance control and billing per waybill generated.
- **Kotemah** — manufacturing: zero-gravity load handling equipment, portable exoskeletons and ergonomic assessment under Mexico's NOM-036-1-STPS-2018 standard.
- **Mileva Dynamics** — manufacturing: an Andon system for presence and productivity control through facial recognition, running at the edge on each workstation's own node.
- **Oxtron** — agrifood and petrochemical: product carbon footprint under IPCC and GHG Protocol methodology, and fixed-source CO₂ capture at up to 90% purity for industrial feedstock.
- **Radek** — cross-sector: digitalization of established companies and venture building, turning in-house development into an additional digital business.
- **Sabia** — industry and services: systems integration, applied AI and automation of processes that still live in spreadsheets.

## The two use cases worked on the floor

The Cluster's stand was built around two situations the Puebla industry faces regularly:

- **The supplier that almost qualifies.** They have the machine and the craft, but evaluations ask for traceability, documented quality control, response times or certifications they cannot yet sustain. It is not a production problem but a systems problem: that is where the Cluster connects them with the talent and startups that implement those systems.
- **The buyer who can't find anyone.** The purchase requirement has no national supplier, or the process to solve is internal and no catalog covers it. In that case you don't search: you build it, and the Cluster acts as the bridge between industry and the startups that can develop it.

## Four entry routes to the Cluster

Over the two days, the stand attended visitors by profile through four engagement routes:

- **Industry, suppliers and corporates** — open innovation, technical talent and corporate venture.
- **Startups and entrepreneurs** — Innovation Hub, first-customer connections and access to investment.
- **Investors** — curated dealflow, co-investment and Pitch Days.
- **Universities and research centers** — internships and job board, technology transfer and the Nodes model.

## The Cluster's role

At the Industrial Clusters Pavilion, the Cluster of Startups and Innovation presented itself as the layer that connects Puebla's established industry with the technology, talent and startups that modernize it. The model operates in four steps: industry challenge, talent and startups, technology solution, and scaling with investment.

Behind that offer stands the Innovation Hub infrastructure at CERHAN II: coworking and meeting rooms, a FabLab, an AI lab with GPUs, specialized laboratories — textiles and biomaterials, water treatment, physicochemical analysis — and Nodes across the state.
