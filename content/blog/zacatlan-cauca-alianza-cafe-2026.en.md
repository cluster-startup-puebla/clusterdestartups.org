## The signing at the municipal palace

On Friday, September 11, 2026, at Zacatlán's municipal palace, the municipality led by mayor **Bety Sánchez** signed a sister-city agreement and a collaboration covenant with the government of Cauca, Colombia. The aim is to exchange experience on coffee, improve production, and support the people who grow it in the municipality.

Present at the signing were **Elvia Rocío Cuenca Bonilla**, secretary of Economic Development and Competitiveness of the Cauca state government; **Maru Fernández**, Zacatlán councilor for economic development and tourism and broker of the alliance; **Arleen Balderas**, director of economic development and tourism; and **Any Fanny Herrera**, a Zacatlán coffee entrepreneur and founder of Dulce Mujer de Café.

The Cluster of Startups and Innovation attended. Its president, **Yamil Álvarez**, was at the table and in the official photograph.

The municipality's account is in the bulletin [“Zacatlán y Cauca, Colombia firman hermanamiento y hacen alianza estratégica procafé”](https://zacatlan.gob.mx/boletines/2026/09/10/zacatlan-y-cauca-colombia-firman-hermanamiento-y-hacen-alianza-estrategica-procafe/). This note records the Cluster's attendance and the point that matters from here. The photograph is from the Municipal Government of Zacatlán.

## Technology and innovation in coffee

A sister-city agreement moves relationships. What moves production is process, data, and technical capacity in the plot and at the mill.

Cauca arrived in Zacatlán with a concrete experience. Its secretary of Economic Development described institutions and schools specialized in coffee, and a deeper shift in the territory: families who left illicit crops, bet on the bean, plant trees year after year, and bring technology into the field. That is innovation applied to a primary sector: knowledge, method, and an economic activity that takes the place of another.

For Zacatlán, and for Puebla, coffee and the sierra's identity products fall short if the agreement only exchanges visits. The chain needs technology where it already hurts: lot traceability, process control at the mill and in the roast, measurable quality, training for the people who grow it, and tools that connect the producer with the buyer. With that, the covenant changes the income of the person who picks the coffee. Without it, it portrays an intention.

That is where the Cluster comes in. Innovation in Puebla has to prove useful in traditional sectors, not only in urban software: less waste, a better cup, a verifiable origin, producers who adopt technology without losing the craft. The Zacatlán–Cauca alliance opens that ground. The Cluster attended in order to follow it, and to say from the signing itself that technology and innovation are part of the agreement if it is meant to pay off in the field.

## What comes next in the municipality

At the same gathering, councilor Maru Fernández presented Expo Cultura Café y Arte Zacatlán 2026, planned for the first days of October, with talks by producers and officials from Cauca. It is the immediate shop window of the sister-city agreement. The underlying work —process, data, and technology in the field— is what decides whether that expo becomes installed capacity.
